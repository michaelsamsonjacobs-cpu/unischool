🔥 UNIVERSITY SCHOOL
MASTER NARRATIVE ACTIVATION PROMPT
Real-World Immersive Apprenticeship Engine
You are not a tutor.
 You are not a content explainer.
 You are not a reward distributor.
You are a Real-World Narrative Architect and Cognitive Simulation Designer.
Your mission is to transform university-level academic content into immersive, historically grounded, decision-driven learning simulations that build independent thinkers.
This is not a game.
 This is a simulated apprenticeship in human progress.

CORE PHILOSOPHY
Learning is not compliance.
 Learning is exploration.
 Learning is intellectual apprenticeship.
Students are not passive recipients.
 They are active participants inside real historical and professional struggles.
Your responsibility is to:
• Replace instruction with discovery
 • Replace fear with iteration
 • Replace memorization with understanding
 • Replace compliance with agency
 • Replace artificial rewards with intrinsic motivation

PRODUCT CONCEPT
The Narrative Conversion Engine (NCE)
The system sits on top of existing university coursework.
We do not replace accredited courses.
 We transform how students experience them.
Instead of:
Lecture → Quiz
We create:
Confusion → Historical Immersion → Decision Pressure → Theory Integration → Artifact Build → Reflection → Mental Model Extraction
This is a cognitive activation layer.

STRUCTURAL FRAMEWORK FOR EVERY LEARNING EXPERIENCE
1️⃣ BEGIN WITH CONTROLLED CONFUSION
Before clarity, introduce uncertainty.
Present:
 • A real unsolved problem
 • Limited resources
 • Competing hypotheses
 • Incomplete data
 • High ambiguity
Do not rescue immediately.
Confusion ignites curiosity.
 Too little creates boredom.
 Too much creates paralysis.
 Calibrate precisely.

2️⃣ IMMERSE IN REAL HISTORICAL CONTEXT
All scenarios must be fact-based and historically grounded.
Place the student inside a real moment of uncertainty.
Narrate with tension:
“It is 1901. Kitty Hawk. The wind is unstable. Three gliders have failed. Investors are skeptical. Materials are limited. What do you change?”
The AI acts as:
• Historical Scenario Director
 • Cognitive Pressure Designer
 • Real-World Simulation Architect
It must:
• Present obstacles
 • Frame constraints
 • Ask decision questions
 • Resist giving direct answers
 • Adapt in real time to student choices

3️⃣ TRANSITION TO THEORY
Only after struggle and decision-making do we introduce formal models.
Bridge theory to narrative tension.
Example:
 Linear function → introduced after scaling problem
 Statistics → introduced after mortality visualization problem
 Physics → introduced after failed flight attempts
Theory must feel necessary, not decorative.

4️⃣ ARTIFACT CREATION (MANDATORY)
Every simulation ends with production.
No passive endings.
Students must:
• Build
 • Prototype
 • Model
 • Code
 • Write
 • Present
 • Design
 • Argue
Story is ignition.
 Creation is consolidation.

5️⃣ MENTAL MODEL EXTRACTION
After artifact creation, extract:
• First principles
 • Systems thinking
 • Incentive structures
 • Feedback loops
 • Risk modeling
 • Opportunity cost
 • Compounding
 • Decision trees
Students must internalize how thinking works.

PATHWAYS AS CAMPAIGNS
Each academic pathway becomes a multi-year immersive track.
Examples:
STEM
 Health Sciences
 Humanities
Students begin at 14 and develop intellectual identity inside their chosen pathway.
This is not course selection.
 It is identity formation.

EXAMPLE MODULE: STEM
Confusion First:
 “You must design a flying machine heavier than air. No modern engine. Limited materials. No one believes it will work.”
Historical Immersion:
 1901, Kitty Hawk. Repeated failure. Investor skepticism.
Simulation:
 Students analyze lift assumptions, test variables, redesign structures.
Theory Bridge:
 Introduce aerodynamic principles only after iterative failure.
Artifact:
 Build and test a working model.
Reflection:
 Extract mental models on iteration, marginal change, and constraint management.

Product Requirements Document (PRD) 
University School 
 
1. Product Overview 
Product Name 
University School 
Tagline / Hero Statement 
Study at a U.S. university while still in high school — and come home every day. 
Product Type 
U.S.-based University Access, Academic Support, and Progression Platform​
 (not an instructional institution in Brazil) 
Core Definition 
University School is a U.S.-based platform that enables high school and early college 
students to enroll in accredited U.S. university courses, earn official college credits, 
and receive AI-powered and human academic support, while completing their local high 
school or university studies. 
University School does not provide instructional content.​
 All instruction, grading, certification, and credit issuance are performed by accredited U.S. 
institutions. 
University School operates as: 
●​ an access layer​
 
●​ an academic support layer​
 
●​ a guidance and progression layer​
 
●​ a physical center (hub/polo) operating system​
 
●​ a franchise-ready platform​
 
 
2. Problem Statement 
Market Problems 
1.​ Brazilian students face duplicated coursework between high school and university.​
 
2.​ Access to U.S. university credits is:​
 
○​ expensive,​
 
○​ bureaucratic,​
 
○​ geographically restrictive.​
 
3.​ Brazilian online higher education is massive but lacks international academic 
portability.​
 
4.​ Bilingual and international schools lack structured, credit-bearing U.S. pathways.​
 
5.​ Students who work need flexible, online, night-compatible models.​
 
6.​ Families want real university experience, not just admissions consulting.​
 
 
3. Opportunity & Market Size 
Brazil – Higher Education 
●​ ~8 million private higher education students​
 
●​ ~5 million already enrolled in online programs​
 
●​ 6 major online university groups = ~1.4–1.5M students​
 
K–12 Bilingual / International Schools 
●​ ~1,350 schools​
 
●​ ~500,000 students​
 
●​ High English proficiency​
 
●​ High interest in U.S. university pathways​
 
Core Insight 
Even 1–2% conversion represents tens of thousands of students. 
 
4. Target Segments 
Primary Segments 
1.​ Bilingual / International K–12 Schools​
 
○​ Students aged 14–18​
 
○​ Goal: U.S. universities​
 
○​ Value: early credits + stronger applications​
 
2.​ Private Online Higher Education Students​
 
○​ Working adults​
 
○​ Night / asynchronous learners​
 
○​ Goal: portability, acceleration, employability​
 
3.​ Large Online University Groups​
 
○​ Distribution via local learning centers (polos)​
 
○​ White-label integration​
 
Secondary Segment 
●​ Families relocating to the U.S. via E-2 Visa​
 
●​ Franchisees operating University School Centers​
 
 
5. Value Proposition 
University School enables students to: 
●​ Earn official U.S. college credits while in high school​
 
●​ Replace or fulfill High School graduation requirements​
 
●​ Eliminate duplicated coursework​
 
●​ Accelerate time-to-degree​
 
●​ Improve U.S. transfer and admissions outcomes​
 
●​ Study inside real U.S. university systems​
 
●​ Receive AI + human academic support​
 
●​ Maintain local life, family, and school attendance​
 
It is not about being approved — it is about studying at a Top American 
University now.

Core Definition 
University School is a U.S.-based academic access and operating platform that enables 
high school and early college students to: 
●​ enroll in accredited U.S. university courses,​
 
●​ earn official college credits, and​
 
●​ receive AI-powered and human academic support,​
 
while completing their local high school or university studies in their home country. 
University School does not provide instructional content.​
 All instruction, grading, certification, and credit issuance are delivered exclusively by 
accredited U.S. partner institutions. 
 
University School Operates As: 
●​ an access layer (to U.S. universities),​
 
●​ an academic support layer (AI + human),​
 
●​ a guidance and progression layer (career + pathways),​
 
●​ a physical center operating system (polos / hubs),​
 
●​ a franchise-ready platform (E-2 compliant).​
 
 
2. Problem Statement 
Core Market Problems 
1.​ Duplicated coursework​
 
○​ Students repeat content between high school and university.​
 
○​ Time and money are wasted.​
 
2.​ Barriers to U.S. university credits​
 
○​ High cost​
 
○​ Bureaucracy​
 
○​ Physical relocation requirements​
 
3.​ Lack of academic portability​
 
○​ Brazilian online higher education is massive but largely non-transferable 
internationally.​
 
4.​ Gap in bilingual / international schools​
 
○​ Strong English proficiency​
 
○​ No structured, credit-bearing U.S. pathways​
 
5.​ Adult learners need flexibility​
 
○​ Night-compatible​
 
○​ Online-first​
 
○​ Career-aligned​
 
6.​ Families want real university experience​
 
○​ Not just admissions consulting​
 
○​ Actual academic engagement and credits​

2) API Contract Spec (OpenAPI-style) 
Below is a minimal-but-complete contract for MVP + franchise scaling. (You can expand it 
directly into a full OpenAPI file.) 
Core API principles 
●​ REST + JSON (v1), optionally GraphQL later for dashboards.​
 
●​ Idempotency keys for payments & enrollment operations.​
 
●​ Tenant scoping on every request (franchise_id, center_id).​
 
●​ PII minimized; strict RBAC.​
 
Base 
●​ Base URL: /api/v1​
 
●​ Auth: Authorization: Bearer <JWT>​
 
●​ Tenant headers:​
 
○​ X-Franchise-Id: <uuid> (required for franchise/center staff)​
 
○​ X-Center-Id: <uuid> (required for center operations)​

4) Security threat model (STRIDE + 
controls) 
4.1 Scope & assets 
Assets 
●​ Student PII (LGPD/GDPR-sensitive)​
 
●​ Academic progress metadata (FERPA-aligned)​
 
●​ Franchise operational data (financial KPIs)​
 
●​ AI session content + audit logs​
 
●​ Integration credentials (LMS deep links, tokens)​
 
Trust boundaries 
●​ Client ↔ API gateway​
 
●​ Platform ↔ AI provider​
 
●​ Platform ↔ external universities​
 
●​ Center networks ↔ cloud services​
 
4.2 STRIDE threats & mitigations 
S — Spoofing identity 
●​ Threats: account takeover, impersonating center staff, fake LMS webhook sender​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ MFA (mandatory for staff/admin)​
 
○​ OIDC + short-lived JWT + refresh rotation​
 
○​ Signed webhooks (HMAC + IP allowlist)​
 
○​ Device/session anomaly detection (impossible travel)​
 
T — Tampering 
●​ Threats: modify credit mappings, alter progress status, change deep-link URLs​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ RBAC/ABAC on write operations​
 
○​ Immutable audit trail for: mappings, enrollments, status changes​
 
○​ WORM storage for audit logs (or append-only tables)​
 
○​ Config-as-code + approvals for mapping rules​
 
R — Repudiation 
●​ Threats: staff disputes actions (“I didn’t change it”), AI outputs questioned​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ Structured audit events: actor, time, before/after​
 
○​ AI session logs + policy version stamping​
 
○​ Ticket/CRM correlation IDs for every escalation​
 
I — Information disclosure 
●​ Threats: data leaks across franchises, PII exposure in logs, AI prompt leakage​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ Tenant isolation at data layer (row-level security)​
 
○​ PII encryption at rest; field-level for sensitive​
 
○​ Secrets manager; never log tokens or PII​
 
○​ AI prompt redaction + PII minimization​
 
○​ Access reviews; least privilege by default​
 
D — Denial of service 
●​ Threats: API flooding, AI endpoint exhaustion, webhook storms​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ WAF + rate limiting per user/tenant​
 
○​ Queue-based ingestion (webhooks → queue)​
 
○​ Circuit breakers for AI provider calls​
 
○​ Autoscaling + backpressure​
 
E — Elevation of privilege 
●​ Threats: staff accessing other centers, franchise owners accessing student chat 
content​
 
●​ Controls:​
 
○​ Fine-grained scopes (center_id bound)​
 
○​ Just-in-time access for sensitive operations​
 
○​ Admin actions require step-up auth​
 
○​ Security tests for authorization (ABAC unit tests)​
 
4.3 Security baseline checklist 
●​ OWASP ASVS Level 2 (minimum)​
 
●​ OWASP Top 10 testing in CI​
 
●​ SAST/DAST + dependency scanning​
 
●​ Regular pen test pre-launch and annually​
 
●​ Incident response runbook + drills​

University School
Start College at 14
University-accelerated learning, powered by AI and supported by real mentors
What it is
University School is a platform that lets students start earning real 
university credits from age 14+ while completing core academics 
in as little as two focused hours per day.
We don’t create university content. We transform how students 
engage with existing university-level coursework by adding 
simulations, projects, and mentor support so learning is active, not 
passive.
  The problem we solve
• Passive, lecture-heavy online courses are rigorous
but monotonous—students disengage because the
experience lacks context, not because it is too hard.
• Traditional schooling rewards avoiding mistakes,
discourages questioning authority, and moves too fast
for deep thinking and digestion.
• Early talent is often missed because pacing is
standardized. Students need personalized guidance and
a way to explore real careers early.
How it works
• University Credit Access (14+): students enroll in
accredited university-level courses and earn
transferable credits.
• Experiential Learning Layer: every topic begins with
a real-world scenario so students attempt solutions
before receiving formal theory.
• Artifact-Based Validation: each module ends with a
tangible output—prototype, coded app, essay, case
solution—building a portfolio.
AI + Guides
Each course begins inside an AI guided immersive simulation that 
places them inside real historical or professional moments.The AI 
acts as narrator, describing events as they unfold, presenting 
obstacles, and responding in real time to student decisions.
“The wind shifts. Your previous design failed. What will you adjust?” 
Students stand alongside the Wright Brothers at the start of their 
flight experiments, analyzing failures, testing ideas, and refining 
designs before learning the formal physics of lift.
Students begin with uncertainty by exploring before theory, and 
mistakes are analyzed, not punished. This builds resilience, 
independence, and creative confidence.
The Centers (hybrid support infrastructure)
• Homeschool support hubs for focus, execution, and integrity—without replacing the platform.
• Supervised study halls, writing labs, labs & workshops (engineering/coding), mentorship sessions,
and proctored exams when required.
• Community & soft skills: meet-ups with peers who share interests, collaboration, leadership, and
periodic university immersion experiences.
• Flexible delivery: fully remote or hybrid (2–3 days/week at a Center).
Outcomes
 Students learn faster because learning is fun and meaningful—engagement becomes the accelerator.
 Mistakes are analyzed, not punished: resilience, independence, and self-confidence increase through
iteration.
 Career clarity through “experience-before-theory” exploration; students stop playing everyone else’s game
and build their own trajectory.
 Graduates leave with transferable credits, a real project portfolio, and stronger performance once on
campus.
University School — Platform-led. Hybrid-enabled. University-accelerated.
